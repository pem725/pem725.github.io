Impact across the whole department

Every full-time faculty member in the George Mason University Department of Psychology — professors, instructors, everyone — measured the same way from two independent bibliometric databases. Two things this shows that a hand-picked list never could: how concentrated the department's research impact really is, and how much any one person's "number" depends on which database you cite. 6 · Relative to the pack experimental

Each faculty's cumulative citations divided by the department median at the same career year. The solid line at 1× is the median itself: above it means pulling ahead of the typical colleague at that stage, below means behind. This strips out both career length and absolute scale, so it asks one honest question — are you out- or under-performing the middle of the pack, and is the gap widening or closing over a career? 7 · Impact per paper experimental

Citations ÷ number of works — influence density, not volume. It separates "publishes a lot" from "publishes work the field actually cites." A long back-catalogue of rarely-cited papers lands low here; a few field-shaping papers land high. (OpenAlex only — it's the source with a clean work count. Field norms differ, so read within rank, not across disciplines.)

8 · Still cited now? experimental

Career citations (horizontal) vs. recent citation rate — average citations received per year since 2020 (vertical). The dashed line is the department's typical ratio of the two. Above it = getting cited more than a back-catalogue that size would predict (still landing); below and to the right = a large body of work that the field has largely stopped citing. A post-COVID pulse check.

9 · Citations by rank experimental

Every faculty member as a dot on their rank's row (log citation axis), with each rank's median marked. Ranks are supposed to reflect a rising research record — so a dot that sits far to the left of its row, down among a more junior rank's range, is the signal worth discussing. The question isn't any one person; it's whether rank and research impact still track each other here.

10 · Coasting vs. climbing experimental

Two things at once: accumulated stature (career citations, horizontal) and current momentum (average citations per year since 2020, vertical). The dashed cross-hairs are the department medians, splitting everyone into four quadrants — Sustained (big and still landing), Rising (early but accelerating), Slowing (a large body of work the field cites less each year), and Quiet. Where you sit — and where the senior ranks sit — is the whole question.
